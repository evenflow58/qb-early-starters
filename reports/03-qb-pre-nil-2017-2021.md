# Pre-NIL Mirror Study: QB Recruiting Classes 2017–2021 vs. Post-NIL 2022–2024

## Summary

Using exactly the post-NIL study's definitions (247Sports Composite **final** rankings, HS recruits only, pro-style + dual-threat combined, "early starter" = ≥6 games started (GS) in a single season in Year 1 or Year 2, starts summed across schools), the pre-NIL classes of 2017–2021 produced:

- **37 blue-chip (4★+5★) early starters from a pool of ~143** — a 25.9% hit rate, statistically indistinguishable from post-NIL (22/86, 25.6%). For 2017–2020 alone: 31/109 (28.4%).
- **51 3-star early starters** (a conservative floor of ~40 individually well-sourced; the rest flagged †), from an estimated 3-star pool of ~700–780 — a hit rate of ~6.5–7%, vs. post-NIL 26/444 (5.9%). Per class, 3-stars produced **10.2 early starters vs. 7.4 for blue chips** — comparable-to-greater raw output in both eras.
- **Capture:** signing schools captured 33/37 (89.2%) of pre-NIL blue-chip early starters and 49/51 (96.1%) of 3-star early starters. Post-NIL, blue-chip capture fell to 17/22 (77.3%) while 3-star capture stayed perfect (26/26).
- **Retention (careers complete):** only 27/81 (33.3%) of pre-NIL early starters with a verified final status never transferred from their signing school (Metric A); 32/81 (39.5%) never transferred from their qualifying school (Metric B). Post-NIL (mid-career snapshot, fall 2026): 22.9% / 27.1%. Retention fell in the NIL era at every star level — **but 3-star early starters' signing-school retention fell the most** (29.5% → 15.4%), undercutting the "3-stars are sticky" premise.
- **Upside (NFL Draft):** 20/37 (54.1%) of pre-NIL blue-chip early starters were drafted (5-star qualifiers 9/11, eight of them in Round 1) vs. 11/51 (21.6%) of 3-star early starters. The upside gap by star level is large and real.

**Overall verdict on the hypothesis: PARTLY SUPPORTED.** Components (i) raw counts and (ii) capture are supported; component (iii) retention is only partly supported and cuts against the hypothesis by star level; component (iv) upside is not supported as stated — 3-star early starters occasionally produced franchise QBs, but blue-chip early starters were drafted at ~2.5× the rate.

**Confidence basis:** blue-chip pools and qualifiers are verified from 247 Composite class pages (index reads, 2026-11-08) with GS cross-checked across Sports-Reference/FootballDB/Wikipedia/school bios. 3-star qualifier counts mix verified and flagged (†) cases. **3-star/2-star pool denominators could not be fully enumerated** (Composite pages paginate past the accessible fetch) and are presented as estimates with a sensitivity range — no headline conclusion changes across that range.

## Methods, definitions, and judgment calls

- **Era split.** Classes 2017–2021 all signed before NIL took effect July 1, 2021. The **2021 class is flagged TRANSITION throughout**: signed Dec 2020/Feb 2021 pre-NIL, but played its entire career under NIL and the one-time free-transfer rule (adopted April 2021). Every headline total is given for 2017–2021 and for 2017–2020.
- **Star determination:** final Composite, not mid-cycle. This matters: Tanner McKee and Matt Corral (2018) were 5-stars mid-cycle but 4-star final; Tua Tagovailoa (2017) earned his 5th star only in the final update (No. 32 overall).
- **Artur Sitkowski (2018) — reclassified to 3-star.** A pre-final (Aug 2017) gallery listed him 4-star (0.9629), but final-Composite sources (Athlon: No. 395 overall / No. 18 pro-style; a 247 transfer article calling him "a former three-star recruit from the class of 2018") place him as a 3-star final. He is counted as a 3-star, signed Rutgers, and is a 3-star early starter (11 GS in 2018). This moves one qualifier from the blue column to the 3-star column.
- **Reclassifications:** Quinn Ewers (2022→2021) and JT Daniels (2019→2018) are counted in the class they signed in. Jake Bentley reclassified out of 2017 into 2016 and is correctly absent.
- **Pool artifacts flagged, not silently dropped:** Jalen Suggs (2020, 4★) signed to play basketball at Gonzaga; Bubba Chandler (2021, 4★) signed with Clemson but went to MLB. Both remain in the 4-star pool denominators (as the post-NIL study treated its lists) and are marked N/A.
- **FCS qualifiers:** Shedeur Sanders (2021, 4★) qualified at Jackson State (13 GS in both 2021 and 2022). He is counted, flagged †FCS, because the post-NIL study's FBS-centric sources (Sports-Reference) would not capture an FCS qualifier the same way. Brevin White (2018, Princeton) did not qualify.
- **COVID-19 (2020 season):** the 6-start threshold is kept for comparability, but the shortened 2020 season bears on the 2019 class's Year 2 and the 2020 class's Year 1. Documented effects: Dylan Morris started all 4 of Washington's 2020 games (GS 4 — could not reach 6); Taulia Tagovailoa started 4 of Maryland's 5; conversely Graham Mertz qualified by starting all 7 of Wisconsin's games, and Ken Seals (9), Will Rogers (exactly 6), Jalon Daniels (exactly 6) and Michael Pratt (8) qualified in 9–10-game team seasons. TJ Finley (2020 class) is the mirror-image near-miss: 5 GS at LSU in 2020.
- **GS sourcing hierarchy:** Sports-Reference CFB gamelogs, cross-checked with Wikipedia GP/GS tables, FootballDB, and official school bios. Where sources conflict, the conflict is flagged and the value used is stated (worked example: Jarret Doege 2017 — Bowling Green bio says 5 starts, Wikipedia's table says 6; immaterial because he started all 12 in 2018 and qualifies either way. Chase Garbers 2018 — FootballDB GS 10, Wikipedia GS 9; qualifies either way; 10 used).
- **Non-qualifier GS values** in Part 1 are given as assessed figures ("0/0", "~2") where playing-time sources showed totals clearly below the threshold (redshirt/backup seasons); every qualifier and every 4–5-start near-miss was individually verified.
- **Retention denominators:** 7 of 88 early starters (all flagged † 3-stars: Isaiah Green, Steven Williams, Seth Boomer, Cornelious Brown, Cameron Friel, Tyler Phommachanh, Brady Olson) have unverified final transfer status. Retention rates are computed on the 81 with verified status; counting all 7 as transfers would lower Metric A to 27/88 (30.7%) and Metric B to 32/88 (36.4%).

## PART 2 — 3-star, 2-star, 1-star QBs, classes 2017–2021

### Denominators — read this caveat first

The 247 Composite class pages paginate ("Load More") past what the web index fetch returns (~first 50 QBs per class), so the blue-chip lists above are complete but **full 3-star and 2-star denominators could not be enumerated player-by-player**. The post-NIL study's enumerated pools averaged ~148 3-stars and ~78–83 2-stars per class, and the pre-NIL Composite QB lists are of comparable total depth. The denominators used below are therefore **estimates**: 3-star ≈ 140–156 per class (≈700–780 over five classes); 2-star ≈ 60–80 per class (≈300–400 over five classes). The resulting 3-star early-starter rate is ~6.5–7.3%; even at the extremes of the plausible range (pool 600 → 8.5%; pool 900 → 5.7%) it remains in the same band as post-NIL's 5.9% and far below the blue-chip rate. No conclusion in Part 4 depends on the exact denominator.

**1-star: no 1-star pool exists in any 2017–2021 Composite QB list examined** — the lowest star category present is 2-star. This matches the post-NIL finding exactly.

**2-star early starters: none verified.** The only candidate who could sit on the 2★/3★ boundary is Steven Williams (Old Dominion, 2017 class, ~10 GS as a 17-year-old true freshman in 2017); his final Composite rating could not be pinned down, and he is carried as a flagged 3-star below. If he was in fact a 2-star, the 2-star count becomes 1 and the 3-star count 50 — immaterial to every rate.

### Component verdicts

**(i) Do 3-stars produce a comparable raw number of early starters? — SUPPORTED.**
In both eras, 3-stars produced *more* early starters per class than all blue chips combined (pre-NIL 10.2 vs. 7.4 per class; post-NIL 8.7 vs. 7.3), despite a per-recruit hit rate roughly one-quarter of the blue-chip rate (~7% vs. ~26%), simply because the 3-star pool is ~5× larger. Even on the conservative floor (~40 verified pre-NIL 3-star qualifiers = 8.0/class), the counts are comparable. A school signing several 3-star QBs should expect, in aggregate, roughly as many early starters as a school signing the same number of blue chips *only if it signs ~4× as many of them* — the raw-count parity is a pool-size effect, not a per-player effect. That qualification matters for roster math but not for the verdict as posed.

**(ii) Does the signing school capture 3-star starters more reliably than blue-chip starters? — SUPPORTED.**
Pre-NIL: 3-star capture 96.1% vs. blue-chip 89.2% (2017–2020: 97.5% vs. 93.5%). Post-NIL: 3-star capture a perfect 26/26 vs. blue-chip 17/22 (77.3%). The era change is concentrated exactly where the hypothesis says: blue-chip capture fell ~16 points from the clean pre-NIL classes to the NIL classes, driven by elite QBs (Sayin-type cases post-NIL; Fields/Ewers/Dart pre-figuring it) taking their qualifying seasons elsewhere, while 3-star capture did not move. This is the strongest component of the hypothesis.

**(iii) Do early starters stay less in the NIL era, and does it differ by star level? — PARTLY SUPPORTED, and the star-level pattern cuts against the hypothesis.**
Direction: yes — Metric A fell from 33.3% (pre-NIL, final) to 22.9% (post-NIL, snapshot that can only decline), and Metric B from 39.5% to 27.1%. But three qualifications: (a) pre-NIL retention was already poor — two-thirds of pre-NIL early starters transferred at least once, because the portal (2018) and graduate transfers predate NIL; the NIL-era drop is an acceleration, not a regime change from loyalty to mobility. (b) By star level, the drop was *larger for 3-stars* (Metric A 29.5% → 15.4%) than for blue chips (37.8% → 31.8%). Whatever makes early starters leave in the NIL era hits the supposed "low downside" group hardest — consistent with 3-star starters transferring *up* for money/stage (Penix, Hartman, Gabriel, Leonard all left their qualifying schools pre-NIL too) and with blue-chip starters who survive to start having already won their job. (c) The transition class behaves intermediately on Metric A (28.6%), consistent with a gradual mobility-regime shift rather than a single NIL switch (see Confounders).

**(iv) What does pre-NIL draft data say about realized upside by star level? — NOT SUPPORTED as stated ("3-stars with high upside" as a general claim); the upside is real but rare and star-dependent.**
Among pre-NIL early starters, 54.1% of blue chips were drafted vs. 21.6% of 3-stars; 5-star early starters went 9/11 drafted with eight first-rounders. The 3-star successes are genuine — Zach Wilson (#2), Michael Penix Jr. (#8), Kenny Pickett (#20), Brock Purdy, Desmond Ridder, Dillon Gabriel — but the *median* 3-star early starter was an undrafted G5 starter (40 of 51 never drafted), and several high-production 3-star starters (Sam Hartman, Kedon Slovis, Grayson McCall) went undrafted entirely. "High upside" describes the top tail of the 3-star distribution, not the typical 3-star early starter. A strategy built on 3-stars buys early starts and capture reliability; it does not buy equivalent NFL-caliber upside per starter produced.

**Overall verdict: PARTLY SUPPORTED.**
The hypothesis is right that, in the NIL era, the *early-starter production a signing school actually keeps* is much closer between 3-stars and blue chips than recruiting rankings imply — because 3-stars match blue chips in raw early-starter output and are captured at ~96–100% while blue-chip capture has slipped to ~77%. It is wrong on its two supporting premises: 3-star early starters are **not** more loyal in the NIL era (their retention fell furthest), and 3-star upside is **not** comparable per player (draft rate ~22% vs. ~54%, first-round rate ~6% vs. ~30% of early starters). A precise restatement the data supports: *"For getting a quarterback on the field early and keeping his qualifying season in-house, a portfolio of 3-star QBs now delivers nearly what blue-chips deliver; for keeping that quarterback for his whole career or getting an NFL-caliber player, star level still matters a great deal."*

## CONFOUNDERS — why "NIL did it" is only partly identifiable

Dated facts (sources in Sources section): the **transfer portal launched October 15, 2018**; **graduate transfers** with immediate eligibility long predate it; the **one-time free-transfer rule was adopted April 2021** (ratified April 28, effective for 2021–22); **NIL took effect July 1, 2021**; and in **August 2020 the NCAA granted all fall athletes a blanket extra year of eligibility** for the COVID season.

Implications:

1. **The "pre-NIL" era is not a pre-mobility era.** Classes 2019–2021 were recruited and played entirely after the portal existed; even the 2017–2018 classes made most of their stay/leave decisions after October 2018. Many pre-NIL transfers in Part 3 were graduate transfers (Hartman, Brewer) or waiver cases (Fields) that needed no NIL and no free-transfer rule. Pre-NIL Metric A of ~33% is the single most important context number in this report: mobility was already the norm.
2. **Retention contrasts conflate three simultaneous changes** (portal 2018, free transfer April 2021, NIL July 2021) plus COVID eligibility. The free-transfer rule plausibly matters more than NIL for the median QB, because it removed the sit-out year — the binding constraint — while NIL money concentrates at the top of the market. Nothing in this design can separate the April 2021 rule from the July 2021 rule for the 2022–24 classes.
3. **COVID eligibility lengthened careers and multiplied transfer opportunities** for the 2017–2021 classes specifically (e.g., Max Johnson starting at a fourth school in a seventh season, 2026; Hartman's sixth-year move). This *inflates* pre-NIL transfer counts relative to a no-COVID pre-NIL baseline — meaning the true pre/post retention gap attributable to the 2021 rule changes is, if anything, larger than the raw gap, but also that pre-NIL careers are not a clean "normal-times" baseline.
4. **Cleanest comparisons, ranked:** (a) *Early-starter production rates* — for the 2017–2019 classes, the Year 1/Year 2 windows predate free transfer entirely, and rankings predate NIL by construction; the near-identity of hit rates across eras (blue ~26–28% both eras; 3-star ~6–7% both eras) is the study's most trustworthy finding, and it says recruiting rankings did not lose predictive power. (b) *Capture* — the qualifying moment is early-career, so capture contrasts (esp. blue-chip 93.5% → 77.3%) are only lightly contaminated, mainly for the 2019–2020 classes whose Year 2 fell in 2020–21. (c) *2021 transition class vs. 2022–24 classes* — both played under portal + free transfer + NIL; they differ mainly in whether NIL existed at signing. The transition class's intermediate Metric A (28.6%, between 34.3% and 22.9%) hints at a modest signing-time NIL effect on top of the transfer-rule effect. (d) *Career retention* is the **least** clean comparison and should carry the least weight in the verdict — which is why component (iii) is only "partly supported."
