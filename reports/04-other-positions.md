# Other-Positions Extension of the QB Recruiting Study — RB, WR, TE, OL, DL, LB, DB
## 247Sports Composite, classes 2017–2021 (pre-NIL mirror) vs 2022–2024 (post-NIL)

**Research date:** 2026-11-08 (Sun). All web facts below are **index** reads (search / text-fetch) on that date unless noted. No figure in this report was verified in the live research browser — see §1.3 for why, and read every number through that limitation.

---

## Summary — read this first

**The full census specified in the mission — every 5-star and 4-star at seven position groups across eight classes individually evaluated for Year-1/Year-2 games started (≈2,800 blue-chips), plus a complete screen of ≈10,000 3-stars — could not be completed or verified from publicly indexed sources in this research pass.** The reasons are documented, not assumed (§1.3): 247Sports' live site blocked the research browser with a client challenge; the web index exposes only the first 50 names of each Composite list; and Sports-Reference CFB's indexed player tables frequently omit the GS column entirely, so starts had to come player-by-player from school bios and GP–GS tables.

What this report therefore delivers, per the mission's own fallback rules ("report a verified FLOOR… instead of presenting them as complete"; "mark the rest as not tracked rather than estimating"):

1. **A verified-floor appendix** of early starters (6+ GS in a single season in Year 1 or Year 2) at every position group, both eras, in which **both** the Composite star level **and** the GS figure are sourced — 9 pre-NIL and 12 post-NIL qualifiers, plus flagged/borderline and instructive non-qualifier cases.
2. **A summary table in the requested shape**, with QB's completed-census numbers in the QB row and **floor / not-tracked (NT) entries — not invented rates — for the other positions.**
3. **Per-position verdicts** on the QB pattern's four components, most of which are honestly **cannot tell** at population level, with two components where the floor + structural evidence does support a directional answer (OL scarcity/development; WR blue-chip immediacy post-NIL).
4. **An overall synthesis** that is conditional: it says where the "recruit 3-stars, buy proven starters" strategy is *consistent with* the verified evidence, where the verified evidence *cuts against* generalizing it, and what a completed census would still need to settle.

**Headline findings that ARE supported by verified evidence:**

- **3-star early starters exist at every skill/defensive position group checked and are not rare curiosities pre-NIL.** The verified pre-NIL 3-star floor (5) actually exceeds the verified pre-NIL blue-chip floor (4) — Jonathan Taylor (RB, 2017), Deuce Vaughn (RB, 2020), Justin Jefferson (WR, 2017), Rashawn Slater (OL, 2017) and Sauce Gardner (DB, 2019) vs Penei Sewell, Peter Skoronski, Kayvon Thibodeaux and Brock Bowers. This mirrors the QB pool-size pattern in direction, but the floors are prominence-selected and **cannot** be converted into rates.
- **Pre-NIL, the verified 3-star early starters were drafted at least as impressively as the verified blue-chip ones** — Jefferson (R1 #22), Slater (R1 #13) and Gardner (R1 #4) were first-rounders, Taylor a 2nd-rounder, Vaughn a 6th-rounder (5/5 drafted), vs 4/4 blue-chips drafted, all in Round 1. That is the opposite shape from the QB upside gap (blue 52.6% vs 3-star 21.6%) — but again, this sample is selected on prominence, which correlates with being drafted, so the population draft-rate comparison is **cannot tell**.
- **Post-NIL, the clearest verified retention failure is a blue-chip, not a 3-star:** Caleb Downs (5-star S, Alabama 2023) started all 14 games as a freshman, then transferred to Ohio State in January 2024 after Nick Saban's retirement. In the verified post-NIL floor, 11 of 12 early starters never transferred from their signing school; Downs is the sole exception. This does **not** replicate the QB finding that the retention drop was largest for 3-stars — but the floor is far too small and biased to overturn it either.
- **OL is the position where the early-starter frame itself is weakest.** Phil Steele's screen of the top-50 OL recruits of 2022 found only **four** full-season starters as true freshmen in the entire class (Will Campbell 13 starts, Kelvin Banks 13, Emery Jones 12, Blake Miller 14) and only seven OL recruits who earned any start at all. Early OL starts are a scarcity event even for blue-chips, in both eras (the pre-NIL verified OL qualifiers — Sewell, Skoronski, Slater — are famous precisely because they were exceptional).
- **TE is the position where the definition bites hardest:** Sam LaPorta (3-star, Iowa 2019) started 2 games in Year 1 and 5 in Year 2 — 7 cumulative, but **not** 6+ in a single season, so he is **not** an early starter under the study's exact definition, despite becoming Iowa's starter in Year 3 and a 2nd-round NFL pick. Cumulative-start counting would misclassify him.
- **GS is a position-distorted metric (new confounder the QB study did not face):** Kayvon Thibodeaux led Oregon in sacks (9) as a true freshman while starting only 5 games (rotation); Nick Singleton "started" only 8 of 13 as a freshman while rushing for 1,061 yards in a two-back rotation; Quinshon Judkins ran for 1,567 yards as a freshman at Ole Miss, yet his Sports-Reference page as indexed does not even expose a GS column. Starts undercount early RB/DL impact in a way they do not for QB, OL or DB.

## 1. Methodology

### 1.1 Definitions reused exactly from the QB study
- **Source of record:** 247Sports Composite final rankings, high-school recruits in the main Composite lists; separate JUCO lists excluded.
- **Position:** the position the player was recruited at, grouped as: RB; WR; TE; OL (OT + IOL/OG + C); DL (interior DL + EDGE/DE); LB (ILB + OLB); DB (CB + S). Kickers, punters, long snappers excluded.
- **Early starter:** 6+ games started (GS) in a **single season** in Year 1 or Year 2 (Year 1 = fall of class year; starts summed across schools if a player transferred mid-window).
- **Eras:** post-NIL = classes 2022–2024; pre-NIL mirror = classes 2017–2021, with **2021 flagged as transition** (signed pre-NIL; played under NIL + the April 2021 free-transfer rule). Brock Bowers (2021) is the one verified qualifier from the transition class and is flagged "2021T" wherever he appears.
- **Capture:** qualifying starts earned at the signing school vs after transferring.
- **Retention, Metric A:** never transferred away from the signing school — full career for pre-NIL classes; status as of fall 2026 for post-NIL classes, with NFL draft/eligibility exhausted counting as "stayed."
- **Upside (pre-NIL only):** NFL draft rate among early starters, blue-chip combined vs 3-star.

### 1.2 ATH handling (as instructed, stated explicitly)
ATH-designated recruits are **excluded** unless 247's position listing places them in one of the seven groups. Applied cases, all sourced:
- **A.J. Dillon (2017)** was listed by the Composite as an **ATH** (No. 24 athlete nationally) — excluded from RB, despite playing RB at Boston College. [247Sports, index]
- **Malaki Starks (2022)** appears as **ATH** in the 2022 Composite overall list — excluded from DB, despite playing safety at Georgia. [247Sports Composite 2022 list, index]
- **Sonny Styles (2022)** appears as **S** in the same list — included in DB, not LB. [247Sports Composite 2022 list, index]
- **Travis Hunter (2022)** appears as **CB** — DB group (his Jackson State Year-1 GS could not be verified in this pass; he is not in the floor counts).
- **Ashton Jeanty (2022)** is the cautionary case for star source discipline: Boise State's bio calls him a "four-star recruit per 247Sports," but the **Composite** line is 3-star, No. 1,284 overall, No. 90 RB — he is counted as a **3-star RB** here, per the study's Composite-only rule. [GatorSports citing Composite; Boise State bio, index]

### 1.3 Why a full census was not achievable in this pass (documented blockers)
1. **247Sports live site:** the research browser received a "Client Challenge" blank page on the Composite rankings URL and rendered no content. Per research rules the challenge was not solved and the site was not retried live; all 247 data here comes from the web index.
2. **Index truncation:** the indexed Composite list returns ranks 1–49 plus a "Load More" control that the index cannot follow. Verified example: the 2022 Composite list (index, 2026-11-08) shows Travis Hunter (CB, Jackson State, .9999) at No. 1 through Derrick Moore (DL, Michigan) at No. 49, then stops. Per-position and per-page lists are likewise truncated, so **exact per-position blue-chip counts could not be extracted** — they are marked NT, not estimated, in §2.
3. **Starts data:** Sports-Reference CFB was specified as the primary GS source, but its indexed player pages do not consistently carry GS — e.g., Quinshon Judkins' indexed Sports-Reference page shows 2022 games, attempts and yards (13 G, 274 att, 1,567 yds) with **no GS column in the rushing/receiving tables as indexed**. GS therefore had to be sourced from official school bios, media-guide G–GS tables and Phil Steele's freshman screens, player by player. That is feasible for prominent players and infeasible, in one pass, for ~2,800 blue-chips.
4. **OL specifically:** as the mission anticipated, offensive linemen barely appear in statistical tables at all; the only class-wide OL starts screen located was Phil Steele's top-50 true-freshman OL review (used for 2022, §4.4). **All OL numbers in this report are floors / lower bounds.**

### 1.5 2-star / 1-star
**No early starter at the 2-star or 1-star Composite level was verified in this pass, and no 2-star census was attempted (as instructed).** One boundary case shows why final Composite stars must be used: T.J. Hockenson was a two-star when he committed to Iowa but "was elevated to a three star by the time he signed" — i.e., a final-Composite 3-star. Absence of a verified 2-star qualifier here is **not** evidence that none exists, especially at OL/DB in the G5.

## 2. Pools

**Exact per-position pool counts (5-star / 4-star / 3-star by group and era) are NOT TRACKED in this pass** — blocker §1.3(2) made the Composite lists uncountable beyond rank 49, and the mission requires blue-chip counts to be exact or flagged, not estimated. The following pool facts **were** verified and bound the problem:

- **Class of 2021 contained 34 five-star recruits** in total (all positions). [Sporting News signing-day tracker, index]
- **Composition of the very top of the 2022 Composite list (ranks 1–49, counted directly from the indexed list, 2026-11-08):** DL 12 (8 DL + 4 EDGE), DB 11 (7 CB + 4 S), OL 8 (6 OT + 2 IOL), QB 6, WR 4, LB 4, RB 3, ATH 1, **TE 0**. Two structural facts fall out of this verified slice: (a) the elite end of a class is dominated by DL/DB/OL, and (b) **TE produces essentially no top-50 recruits** — the TE blue-chip pool is small and drawn from much lower overall ranks than the other groups' pools, which mechanically caps TE early-starter counts per class regardless of era.
- **Blue-chip definition anchor:** the Blue-Chip Ratio literature (Bud Elliott, CBS/247Sports) defines blue-chips as 4/5-stars on the 247Sports Composite, manually checked per signee, and states blue-chips are "10 times more likely to be drafted than two and three star prospects" — the background rate against which the QB study's, and this study's, upside comparisons sit. [247Sports, index]
- **3-star denominators:** not estimated in this report. The QB study estimated them from pagination; pagination was not accessible here (§1.3), so any 3-star rate computed from these floors would have an unverified denominator **and** a floor numerator. **No early-starter RATES are presented for non-QB positions. Any rate a reader computes from the floors below is a lower bound on the numerator over an unknown denominator and should not be quoted as a rate.**

## 4. Per-position findings

### 4.1 RB — confidence: medium-low
**Verified qualifiers.** Pre-NIL, 3-star: **Jonathan Taylor** (2017, Wisconsin) — Composite 3-star, No. 371 overall / No. 24 RB; started 13 of 14 games as a freshman per one 247Sports account and "all 14" per another (discrepancy flagged; ≥6 either way), 1,977 rushing yards; never transferred; drafted 2020 Round 2. **Deuce Vaughn** (2020, Kansas State) — 3-star; Year-1 line 10 GP / **7 GS** in the COVID-shortened 2020 season; never transferred; drafted 2023 Round 6. Post-NIL, blue-chip: **Nick Singleton** (2022, Penn State) — Composite No. 31 overall; started **8** games as a true freshman (played 13), 1,061 yards in a two-back rotation with Kaytron Allen; never transferred. Post-NIL, 3-star: **Ashton Jeanty** (2022, Boise State) — Composite 3-star (see §1.2); 2 starts in Year 1, **10 starts in Year 2 (2023)**, qualifying in Year 2 at his signing school; never transferred; drafted 2025 Round 1, #6 overall.
**Unresolved / excluded:** Quinshon Judkins (2022, Ole Miss, 3-star) — 1,567 freshman rushing yards but GS not verifiable from indexed sources, and he later transferred to Ohio State; excluded from counts, retained in the text because he is exactly the capture/retention case a census must catch. Ollie Gordon II (2022, Oklahoma State) — described by a 247Sports/CBS article as having "signed… in 2022 as a three-star prospect"; his Year-2 starter status is well documented narratively but exact GS was not sourced in this pass; excluded from counts.
**Position note:** RB is where "starts" least equals "early impact" — first snaps of a series go to one back in a rotation (Singleton/Allen; Judkins behind a transfer starter early in 2022). A GS-based early-starter rate will systematically understate early RB production relative to QB/OL/DB, in both eras, for both star bands.

### 4.2 WR — confidence: medium
**Verified qualifiers.** Pre-NIL, 3-star: **Justin Jefferson** (2017, LSU) — official LSU bio G–GS table: 2017 7–0, **2018 13–12**, 2019 15–15; qualifies in Year 2 at his signing school; never transferred; drafted 2020 Round 1, #22. Post-NIL, blue-chip (all Year-1 qualifiers, all at signing schools, none transferred through fall 2026): **Luther Burden III** (2022, Missouri, 5-star, Composite No. 3 overall) — 10 starts in 13 games as a freshman; drafted 2025 Round 2. **Tetairoa McMillan** (2022, Arizona, 4-star) — **9 starts** in 12 games per Phil Steele's freshman screen (a secondary source says 12; flagged); drafted 2025 Round 1, #8. **Jeremiah Smith** (2024, Ohio State, 5-star, No. 1 overall recruit of 2024) — **15 starts** in 16 games as a freshman. **Ryan Williams** (2024, Alabama, 5-star) — **13 starts** in 13 games as a freshman.
**Pattern in the floor:** post-NIL WR is the one group where verified blue-chip Year-1 starters are abundant and immediately productive at playoff-level programs, while no post-NIL 3-star WR qualifier met the verification standard in this pass (the pre-NIL era produced Jefferson). That asymmetry is consistent with WR being an early-rotation, traits-visible position where blue-chip talent starts at once — and it is the opposite raw-count shape from QB. It is a floor asymmetry, not a measured rate, but it is the strongest directional signal in this report after OL.

### 4.3 TE — confidence: low
**Verified qualifier.** **Brock Bowers** (2021T — transition class, Georgia, 4-star) — 15 GP / **13 GS** as a true freshman, 14 starts in Year 2; never transferred; drafted 2024 Round 1. He is the exception that proves the TE rule: he started immediately at a championship program largely because the two blue-chip TEs ahead of him (Darnell Washington, injury; Arik Gilbert, absence) were unavailable — opportunity, not a normal TE development curve.
**Verified non-qualifier (instructive).** **Sam LaPorta** (2019, Iowa, 3-star, No. 42 TE in his class) — Year 1: 12 GP, **2 starts**; Year 2 (2020, Iowa played only 8 games): **5 starts**; Year 3: 13 starts and the full-time job; drafted 2023 Round 2, #34, having never transferred. Under the study's per-season definition he is **not** an early starter. Any TE analysis that sums starts across Years 1–2, or that ignores the COVID-shortened 2020 season, will misclassify exactly the developmental 3-star TEs the strategy debate is about.
**Post-NIL TE:** no qualifier met the verification standard in this pass (e.g., LSU's Mason Taylor was documented as one of three true-freshman starters on LSU's 2022 offense, but his Composite star level was not sourced in this pass, so he is excluded from counts). **TE post-NIL is effectively not tracked** beyond the structural point in §2: with zero TEs in the Composite top 50, the blue-chip TE pool per class is small, so even a high TE blue-chip early-start rate would yield few starters per class.

### 4.4 OL — confidence: medium as a floor; all figures are lower bounds
**Verified qualifiers.** Pre-NIL, blue-chip: **Penei Sewell** (2018, Oregon, 4-star, recruited as an OG) — started **all 7 games he played** as a freshman (missed six through injury), 13 of 14 in Year 2; never transferred; drafted 2021 Round 1, #7. **Peter Skoronski** (2020, Northwestern, 4-star) — "started every game at left tackle as a true freshman in 2020" (COVID-shortened season); never transferred; drafted in the first round in 2023. Pre-NIL, 3-star: **Rashawn Slater** (2017, Northwestern, 3-star, recruited as an OG) — **12 starts** at right tackle as a true freshman, 14 in Year 2; never transferred (opted out of the 2020 COVID season); drafted 2021 Round 1, #13. Post-NIL, blue-chip (all 2022, all Year-1, all at signing schools, none transferred): **Will Campbell** (LSU) — 13 starts at LT as a freshman; LSU's bio notes he started all 38 games in which he appeared across his career; drafted 2025 Round 1. **Kelvin Banks Jr.** (Texas, Composite No. 33 overall) — started all **13** games at LT as a freshman. **Emery Jones** (LSU) — **12 starts** as a freshman per Phil Steele's screen (star level treated as blue-chip, flagged as not independently re-verified in this pass).
**Class-wide screen (the single most important OL fact in this report):** Phil Steele's review of the **top-50 OL recruits of 2022** found just **four** full-season starters among them as true freshmen — Campbell (13), Banks (13), Jones (12) and Clemson's Blake Miller (14; star level unresolved in this pass) — and only **seven** 2022 OL recruits who earned any start at all. Even at the very top of the OL pool, a Year-1 starting job is a ~top-of-class event; for the median blue-chip OL, starts arrive in Year 3+. **Any OL early-starter rate in either era is therefore structurally low, and the QB-style comparison (which treats Years 1–2 as the window) systematically disadvantages OL-heavy recruiting strategies in appearance while saying little about their Year-3–5 value.**

### 4.5 DL — confidence: low
**Verified qualifiers.** Pre-NIL, blue-chip: **Kayvon Thibodeaux** (2019, Oregon, 5-star, Composite No. 2 overall) — Year 1: **5 starts** in 14 games (**not** a qualifier that year, despite a team-high 9 sacks as a rotational rusher); Year 2 (2020): **started all 7 games** of Oregon's COVID-shortened season, qualifying in Year 2; never transferred; drafted 2022 Round 1, #5. Post-NIL, blue-chip: **Mason Graham** (2022, Michigan) — **Composite 4-star, No. 247 overall** (he was a 3-star only on ESPN's separate ratings, a distinction two secondary sources blur); rotation in Year 1, **13 starts in 13 games in Year 2 (2023)**; never transferred; drafted 2025 Round 1, #5.
**Position note:** DL is the second-worst fit for a GS metric after RB: fronts rotate by design, and a 5-star's Year-1 value (Thibodeaux's 9 sacks on 5 starts) can be elite while GS says "not a starter." DL floors in both eras and both star bands are certainly undercounts; no DL rate, capture or retention figure beyond the two cases above is asserted.

### 4.6 LB — confidence: low; pre-NIL not tracked
**Verified qualifier (post-NIL only).** **Harold Perkins Jr.** (2022, LSU, 5-star, Composite No. 8 overall / No. 2 LB) — Year 1: 14 GP, **8 GS**; Year 2: **13 GS** in 13 games; never transferred (LSU 2022–2025). **No pre-NIL LB qualifier, and no LB 3-star qualifier in either era, met the verification standard in this pass. LB pre-NIL is reported as not tracked rather than filled with remembered names whose GS could not be sourced.** Given that LB is a mid-size pool with heavy special-teams/rotation usage in Years 1–2, this is the position group where a completed census is most needed and least substitutable by case evidence.

### 4.7 DB — confidence: medium-low
**Verified qualifiers.** Pre-NIL, 3-star: **Ahmad "Sauce" Gardner** (2019, Cincinnati, 3-star CB) — inserted into the starting lineup late in Year 1 (one secondary source credits him with starting the last 6 games); unambiguously a qualifier in Year 2, **starting 9 games in the COVID-shortened 2020 season** as the established starter; never transferred; drafted 2022 Round 1, #4. Post-NIL, 3-star: **Nick Emmanwori** (2022, South Carolina, S) — 3-star Composite (No. 435 overall per 247Sports); **started the final 12 games** of his freshman season, led the team with 85 tackles, Freshman All-American; never transferred; drafted in 2025. His teammate **DQ Smith** started the final 11 games of 2022 (first eight at nickel, last three at safety) per 247Sports — included in discussion as a **flagged** second 3-star DB qualifier, his Composite star level not independently re-verified in this pass. Post-NIL, blue-chip: **Caleb Downs** (2023, Alabama, 5-star S, No. 6 overall prospect in 2023 per 247Sports) — **started all 14 games** as a freshman, SEC Freshman of the Year, led Alabama with 107 tackles; **transferred to Ohio State in January 2024 following Nick Saban's retirement.**
**Why DB matters disproportionately:** DB supplies the report's cleanest paired contrast. Capture for Downs is 100% — every qualifying start came at his signing school — yet retention is 0%: Alabama developed a Year-1 star and lost him before Year 2 for a reason (a legendary head coach's retirement) that has nothing to do with NIL money per se. Meanwhile the two verified post-NIL 3-star DBs at a non-blueblood (South Carolina) both stayed. That is a single-program anecdote, not a rate — but it shows the QB study's capture/retention divergence (capture falls less than retention) operating at DB in its purest form.

## 5. Per-position verdicts — does the QB pattern hold?

QB pattern components: **(i)** 3-stars produce more early starters per class than blue-chips (pool-size effect); **(ii)** signing-school capture fell post-NIL for blue-chips (93.5% → 77.3%) while 3-star capture stayed ~96–100%; **(iii)** career retention of early starters was already low pre-NIL (33.3%) and fell post-NIL (22.9%), with the drop largest for 3-stars (29.5% → 15.4%); **(iv)** pre-NIL NFL upside among early starters was much higher for blue-chips (52.6%) than 3-stars (21.6%).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RB** | **Partly supported** — verified 3-star qualifiers in both eras (Taylor, Vaughn; Jeanty) alongside blue-chip ones (Singleton); pool-size logic applies, but GS distortion at RB corrupts any count | **Cannot tell** — floor sample is 100% by construction; the Judkins case proves transfer-after-qualifying exists post-NIL | **Cannot tell** | **Cannot tell** — floor sample: 3-stars 2/2 drafted, but prominence-selected |

## 6. Overall synthesis

**Where the strategy generalises (consistent with the verified evidence):**
- **The pool-size premise is real at RB and DB.** In both eras, verified 3-star early starters at RB (Taylor, Vaughn, Jeanty) and DB (Gardner, Emmanwori) reached the very top of the sport — a Heisman-finalist-level back, a #4 overall draft pick, a #6 overall pick — without transferring. A program signing many 3-star RBs/DBs is buying many lottery tickets at positions with short development curves and heavy rotation, where Year-1/2 opportunity is genuinely available. That is the QB pattern's strongest export.
- **Retention risk post-NIL is not a 3-star-specific phenomenon outside QB.** The one verified post-NIL retention loss in this entire study is a 5-star (Downs), driven by a coaching change. Roster-building that assumes "blue-chips stay, 3-stars leave" (or the reverse) is not supported by anything verified here; the QB study's 3-star retention collapse (29.5% → 15.4%) should be treated as a QB finding until a census shows otherwise.

**Where it breaks down:**
- **WR (post-NIL):** the verified early starters are blue-chips starting as true freshmen at Missouri, Arizona, Ohio State and Alabama. At a position where the best 17–18-year-olds now start immediately and are visible doing so, "recruit 3-stars for volume" does not reproduce the QB arithmetic in the floor evidence — the portal half of the strategy (buy proven starters) looks relatively stronger at WR, and homegrown volume looks weaker.
- **OL and TE (both eras): developmental positions.** Early starts are scarce events (four full-season true-freshman starters among 2022's top-50 OL recruits; LaPorta needing until Year 3 despite an NFL trajectory). For OL/TE, the Years-1–2 window measures almost nothing about a signing class's eventual value; a 3-star-volume strategy cannot be evaluated — or executed for early returns — on this clock. What the verified cases do show (Slater, Skoronski, Sewell; Bowers by opportunity, LaPorta by Year 3) is that OL/TE value concentrates in Years 3–5, which is precisely the window the portal can also buy. The honest conclusion for OL/TE is not "3-stars win" or "blue-chips win" but "early-starter metrics are the wrong instrument here."
- **DL and LB: the instrument, not the strategy, fails.** Rotation (DL) and data absence (LB) mean this study cannot say whether the QB pattern holds. Any roster conclusion for DL/LB drawn from GS-based early-starter counts — in either direction — would be unfounded on current evidence.

**How position differences change the roster-building conclusion:** the QB verdict ("partly supported — strongest on capture and raw production, weakest on retention and NFL upside") does **not** transplant as a single verdict. Transplanted position-by-position from the verified evidence: at **RB/DB**, the raw-production leg is at least as strong as at QB and the upside leg looks weaker (3-star early starters reaching Round 1 in the verified cases); at **WR**, the raw-production leg reverses post-NIL in the floor evidence; at **OL/TE**, all four legs are dominated by development curves that push value outside the measurement window; at **DL/LB**, the measurement itself is not yet adequate. A program acting on the QB study alone would over-apply the strategy at WR, mis-time it at OL/TE, and have no evidence at DL/LB.

## 7. Confounders

**Carried over from the QB study:**
- **Transfer portal launched 15 October 2018** — so even "pre-NIL" classes 2019–2021 played with a portal, and 2017–2018 classes gained one mid-career. [BestColleges; ESPN explainer, index]
- **One-time free-transfer rule, April 2021** (ratified 28 April 2021) and **NIL from 1 July 2021** — the 2021 class signed pre-NIL but played entirely under both, hence its transition flag; Bowers (2021T) is retained in the pre-NIL mirror for comparability with the QB study and flagged everywhere. [BestColleges; ESPN, index]
- **COVID eligibility and shortened seasons** — the extra eligibility year stacked rosters with super-seniors in 2021–2022 (suppressing Year-1/2 starts for the 2021 and 2022 classes), and the shortened **2020 season compressed the GS ceiling** for the 2019 class (Year 2) and 2020 class (Year 1): Iowa played 8 games in 2020 (LaPorta's maximum possible GS was 8; he recorded 5, in a split role), Oregon played 7 (Thibodeaux started all 7), Kansas State 10 (Vaughn started 7). A 6+ GS threshold is a materially higher bar in a 7–10-game season than in a 13–15-game season. **Era comparisons that include 2020-affected seasons are biased against the pre-NIL mirror's later classes.**

**Position-specific (new in this study):**
- **GS is not a uniform currency.** It is near-complete information for OL and DB, partial for WR/TE (formation-dependent: a TE in 12-personnel vs a WR3), and actively misleading for RB (series-rotation "starts": Singleton 8 GS with 1,061 yards; Judkins' freshman GS not even exposed by Sports-Reference as indexed) and DL (package rotation: Thibodeaux, 9 sacks on 5 starts).
- **Early NFL entry at RB/DB** compresses careers to three years: "never transferred" is easier to satisfy, and post-NIL retention snapshots for the 2024 class (e.g., Smith, Williams — both still at their signing schools in fall 2026) are provisional, since neither has yet faced a draft/portal decision point after Year 3.
- **Coaching-change transfers** (Downs, after Saban's retirement) are retention failures that correlate with blue-chip status — blue-chips cluster at bluebloods, whose coaching changes are the highest-profile — rather than with NIL bidding alone. Capture/retention analyses that do not control for head-coach departure will misattribute these.
- **Composite-vs-service star confusion** is a live data hazard at exactly the margins that matter: Jeanty (Composite 3-star, 247Sports-own 4-star), Graham (Composite 4-star, ESPN 3-star), Singleton (Composite No. 31, described as a five-star via 247Sports' own ratings). Every count in a completed census must be built from the Composite column only, as the QB study specified.
- **FCS signings in the pool:** Hunter (Jackson State) and other FCS-signed recruits sit inside the main Composite lists; their starts are the least documented of all, biasing any floor against small-school signees of both star bands.
